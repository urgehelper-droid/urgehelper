Porn, specifically

Psychotherapy for problematic pornography use: A comprehensive meta-analysisLópez-Pinar, Esparza-Reig & Bőthe · Journal of Behavioral Addictions · 2025

Across 20 studies and 2,021 people, therapy cut problematic use and how often people watched by a large amount.

A randomized controlled study of group-administered cognitive behavioral therapy for hypersexual disorder in menHallberg et al. · Journal of Sexual Medicine · 2019

In 137 men, seven weeks of CBT reduced symptoms more than waiting did, and the gains held six months later. The trigger and planning steps here borrow from it.

Acceptance and commitment therapy for problematic internet pornography use: A randomized trialCrosby & Twohig · Behavior Therapy · 2016

ACT cut viewing by 93%, against 21% on a waitlist. Noticing urges and thoughts without obeying them is at the heart of it.

Hands-off: Feasibility and preliminary results of a two-armed randomized controlled trial of a web-based self-help tool to reduce problematic pornography useBőthe et al. · Journal of Behavioral Addictions · 2021

A six-week online self-help program reduced problematic use and cravings. Its modules on triggers, risky situations and thinking traps shaped this session.

Treatments and interventions for compulsive sexual behavior disorder with a focus on problematic pornography use: A preregistered systematic reviewAntons et al. · Journal of Behavioral Addictions · 2022

A review of 24 studies. Treatment helped, and cognitive behavioral therapy had the clearest support.

What led here

Why do people watch pornography? The motivational basis of pornography useBőthe et al. · Psychology of Addictive Behaviors · 2021

Using porn to cope with stress, boredom or bad feelings was tied to problematic use. That’s why what set you off picks your path.

“Pornographic binges” as a key characteristic of males seeking treatment for compulsive sexual behaviorsWordecha et al. · Journal of Behavioral Addictions · 2018

In a 10-week diary study, binges followed low mood, stress and anxiety, and each man’s triggers were different.

Background sound

Effects of music interventions on stress-related outcomes: a systematic review and two meta-analysesde Witte et al. · Health Psychology Review · 2020

Across 104 randomized trials and almost 10,000 people, music lowered stress, both in the body and in how stressed people felt. That’s why a calm drone plays under every step. You can mute it anytime.

Dynamic interactions between musical, cardiovascular, and cerebral rhythms in humansBernardi et al. · Circulation · 2009

Swells in music pushed heart rate and blood pressure up, and fades brought them down. So the sound here never builds. It only sways slowly, about every 10 seconds, the same pace as the breathing step.

The urge’s reasons

Problematic internet pornography use: The role of craving, desire thinking, and metacognitionAllen, Kannis-Dymand & Katsikitis · Addictive Behaviors · 2017

Dwelling on the urge and talking yourself into it fed craving. Naming those thoughts as the urge talking is the counter.

What matters

Targeting mechanisms for problematic pornography use interventionsTodorovic, Huisman & Ostafin · Sexual Health & Compulsivity · 2023

After a porn craving was set off in 711 people, briefly bringing something meaningful to mind lowered it more than willpower, mindfulness or awe.

Check in

Putting feelings into words: Affect labeling disrupts amygdala activity in response to affective stimuliLieberman et al. · Psychological Science · 2007

Naming a feeling reduced the brain’s alarm response to emotional images.

Move

The acute effects of physical activity on cigarette cravings: systematic review and meta-analysis with individual participant dataHaasova et al. · Addiction · 2013

Pooling over 15 trials, short bursts of activity cut cravings sharply, and harder effort worked best. Tested with smokers.

Think it through

Prefrontal–striatal pathway underlies cognitive regulation of cravingKober et al. · PNAS · 2010

Thinking about the long-term downsides of giving in cut cravings for cigarettes and food by about a third, and calmed the brain’s reward response.

Training in cognitive strategies reduces eating and improves food choiceBoswell, Sun, Suzuki & Kober · PNAS · 2018

Practising the same long-term thinking reduced how much people actually ate afterwards, not just how much they wanted it.

Walk it off

Walking reduces cue-elicited cigarette cravings and withdrawal symptoms, and delays ad libitum smokingTaylor & Katomeri · Nicotine & Tobacco Research · 2007

A 15-minute brisk walk cut cravings, blunted cravings set off by cues, and people waited almost an hour longer before smoking.

How strong it is

Emotion-regulation choiceSheppes, Scheibe, Suri & Gross · Psychological Science · 2011

Facing mild emotions, people chose to think them through. Facing strong ones, they chose distraction.

Emotion regulation choice: A conceptual framework and supporting evidenceSheppes et al. · Journal of Experimental Psychology: General · 2014

Distraction worked better than rethinking at high intensity, because rethinking needs mental bandwidth a strong emotion uses up.

Somewhere else

Replacing craving imagery with alternative pleasant imagery reduces craving intensityKnäuper et al. · Appetite · 2011

Vividly imagining a pleasant activity lowered craving more than a neutral imagery task. Tested on food cravings.

Write it out

Does monitoring goal progress promote goal attainment? A meta-analysis of the experimental evidenceHarkin et al. · Psychological Bulletin · 2016

Across 138 studies, tracking your progress helped people reach goals, and the effect was larger when it was written down.

Experimental disclosure and its moderators: A meta-analysisFrattaroli · Psychological Bulletin · 2006

Across 146 studies, writing about thoughts and feelings had a small but reliable benefit.

Strong urges

The acute effects of physical activity on cigarette cravings: Exploration of potential moderators, mediators and physical activity attributes using individual participant dataHaasova et al. · Psychopharmacology · 2014

Pooling 19 trials and 930 people, moderate activity cut cravings far more than light activity, and going vigorous added nothing extra.

Hold it

Acute effect of isometric exercise on desire to smoke and tobacco withdrawal symptomsUssher, West, Doshi & Sampuran · Human Psychopharmacology · 2006

Even static muscle tensing reduced the desire to smoke, which is why strong urges get a silent hold.

Stack

Effects of working memory on naturally occurring cravingsDodds et al. · Behaviour Research and Therapy · 2019

A demanding mental task cut real-life cravings more than simply watching a video did.

Playing Tetris decreases drug and other cravings in real world settingsSkorka-Brown, Andrade, Whalley & May · Addictive Behaviors · 2015

In everyday life, a few minutes of Tetris reduced cravings, including cravings for sex, by around 14 points out of 100.

Playing ‘Tetris’ reduces the strength, frequency and vividness of naturally occurring cravingsSkorka-Brown, Andrade & May · Appetite · 2014

Three minutes of Tetris cut craving strength by about a fifth compared with a loading screen.

Stack and follow the dot

Imaginary relish and exquisite torture: The elaborated intrusion theory of desireKavanagh, Andrade & May · Psychological Review · 2005

Cravings are built from vivid mental pictures. Tasks that tie up your visual imagination leave less room for them.

Follow the dot

Desensitizing addiction: Using eye movements to reduce the intensity of substance-related mental imagery and cravingLittel, van den Hout & Engelhard · Frontiers in Psychiatry · 2016

Side-to-side eye movements while holding a craving image made the image less vivid and the craving weaker.

Breathe

Brief structured respiration practices enhance mood and reduce physiological arousalBalban et al. · Cell Reports Medicine · 2023

Exhale-focused breathing improved mood and lowered physical arousal.

Ride it out

Surfing the urge: Brief mindfulness-based intervention for college student smokersBowen & Marlatt · Psychology of Addictive Behaviors · 2009

Smokers taught to ride out urges smoked less afterwards, even when the urge itself didn’t shrink.

Relative efficacy of mindfulness-based relapse prevention, standard relapse prevention, and treatment as usual for substance use disordersBowen et al. · JAMA Psychiatry · 2014

In a trial of 286 people, mindfulness-based relapse prevention cut drug use and heavy drinking over a year.

A pilot study of mindfulness-based relapse prevention for compulsive sexual behaviour disorderHolas et al. · Journal of Behavioral Addictions · 2020

Thirteen men with compulsive sexual behavior spent much less time on porn after eight weeks of the same approach.

Don’t fight it

Paradoxical effects of thought suppressionWegner, Schneider, Carter & White · Journal of Personality and Social Psychology · 1987

Trying not to think about something brought it back more often. That’s why these steps redirect the urge instead of suppressing it.

Lock it in

Implementation intentions and goal achievement: A meta-analysis of effects and processesGollwitzer & Sheeran · Advances in Experimental Social Psychology · 2006

Across 94 tests, deciding in advance “if this happens, I’ll do that” made people much more likely to follow through.

After a slip

First lapses to smoking: Within-subjects analysis of real-time reportsShiffman et al. · Journal of Consulting and Clinical Psychology · 1996

People who felt like giving up after a first slip slipped again sooner. That’s why a slip pauses your count instead of erasing it.

No shame here

Pornography problems due to moral incongruence: An integrative model with a systematic review and meta-analysisGrubbs et al. · Archives of Sexual Behavior · 2019

Shame and moral conflict made people feel more addicted, apart from how much they actually watched. That’s why nothing here shames you.

Read this before you trust the numbers

  • The individual techniques have evidence. This exact sequence hasn’t been tested as a whole.
  • Most of the craving research is on smoking, alcohol and food. The mechanisms likely carry over, but treat effect sizes as estimates.
  • These steps shorten urges and make them easier to ride out. They don’t make them disappear.
  • A self-help tool, not medical treatment.
